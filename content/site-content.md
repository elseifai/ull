Academics: Our approach
Children understand before they memorise, do before they are told, and reflect before they move on.

ULL Auremont follows the CBSE curriculum, shaped by the National Curriculum Framework for School Education (NCF 2023). Our curriculum moves through the four stages the framework sets out: Foundational, Preparatory, Middle and Secondary.

What we believe
• Understanding over rote learning. Every lesson builds the why and the how, not just the what.
• Learning by doing. Children explore, question and discover.
• Skills as well as knowledge. Communication, critical thinking, collaboration and creativity are built into every unit.
• Values in every lesson, taught through stories, real dilemmas and discussion.
• Rooted in India, open to the world.
• A culture of reading and writing in every subject.
The Auremont Learning Cycle
Every lesson, in every subject and grade, follows five steps.

Step
What happens
1. Connect
A question, story, picture or puzzle sparks curiosity and links to what children already know
2. Experience
Children explore hands-on: they handle materials, investigate, experiment and work in groups
3. Clarify
Children explain what they found in their own words; the teacher builds the correct concept and terms
4. Apply
Children use the learning to solve a new problem, design something or argue with evidence
5. Reflect
Children say or write what they learned and what is still unclear; the teacher uses this to plan the next lesson
The five steps stay the same from Nursery to Grade 10. What changes is the form: play, rhymes and objects in the Foundational Stage; experiments and group tasks in the Preparatory Stage; investigations and debates in the Middle Stage; case studies, research and board-style problems in the Secondary Stage.

Indian roots: a lens, not a subject
Indian knowledge is woven into lessons through the Learning Cycle. A lesson may connect through an Indian story or Karnataka example, clarify through the work of an Indian mathematician or scientist, and apply to a real problem in our own city. Indian and world knowledge are always taught side by side, and every example is checked for accuracy.

In every Auremont classroom, you will see
• Children talking, investigating and explaining for most of the lesson.
• Open questions, and time to think before answering.
• Every child taking part, not only the confident few.
• New vocabulary every day, and reading and writing in every subject.
• A link to real life, to values or to India in every lesson.
Every learner, every lesson
A child who finds a concept hard gets smaller steps, visual support and more check-ins. A child ready for more gets deeper challenges, not just more of the same.

Academics: Foundational Stage (Nursery to Grade 2)
Learning through play, stories and wonder.

The first five years build the foundations for everything that follows: language, number sense, curiosity, confidence and the joy of learning. In Nursery, LKG and UKG, children learn through play, toys, rhymes, stories, conversation and movement, with no formal textbooks. In Grades 1 and 2, books are introduced gently, and play remains at the heart of the day.

Five areas of development
• Physical development: running, balancing, throwing and catching; holding a crayon, cutting and threading; healthy habits and safety.
• Socio-emotional and ethical development: making friends, sharing, taking turns, naming feelings, caring for others.
• Cognitive development: observing, sorting, comparing, counting, noticing patterns and asking questions.
• Language and literacy: listening, speaking, sounds and letters, early reading and writing.
• Aesthetic and cultural development: drawing, music, dance, drama and the stories and festivals of India.
Together with good learning habits such as attention, curiosity and persistence, these follow the National Curriculum Framework for the Foundational Stage.

What children learn
• Languages: English and Hindi through rhymes, stories, picture talk and phonics; Kannada from Grade 1. Themes children read and talk about include my family and me, life around us, food and seasons.
• Mathematics: numbers and counting with real objects, shapes, patterns, comparing, measuring, and adding and subtracting through stories and games.
• Our world: my body and senses, my home and neighbourhood, plants and animals, seasons and water, festivals, transport and community helpers.
• Arts and movement: drawing, colouring, clay, music, dance and free play every day.
Each day
Each day opens with Circle Time around a weekly theme: a song, a conversation about a real object or picture, a story children help to tell, a rhyme and a movement game. Short, active sessions in language, number and our world follow. Letters are traced in the air and in sand before they are written, and numbers are counted with beads and blocks before they are written.

By the end of Grade 2, your child will
• Read short, simple texts with understanding, and write simple sentences.
• Count, read and write numbers, and add and subtract with confidence.
• Speak with confidence in a group, and listen to others.
• Look after their own belongings, follow routines and play fairly.
Indian roots in the Foundational Stage
• Indian rhymes, folk tales and traditional games, alongside English ones.
• Festivals, national symbols and Karnataka’s culture, explored through stories, songs and celebrations.
• Kannada and Hindi heard, sung and spoken from the start.
How progress is shared
There are no tests in the early years. Teachers observe each child, keep samples of their work and share a holistic progress report covering every area of development.

Academics: Preparatory Stage (Grades 3 to 5)
From play to purposeful learning.

In Grades 3 to 5, children move from learning through play to more structured learning, while keeping it active, hands-on and joyful. They become fluent readers, confident with numbers and curious about the world around them.

The World Around Us (EVS)

In the classroom
Children carry out simple experiments, work in groups, solve real-life word problems and create something in every unit. Reading corners and vocabulary logs build a love of reading.

By the end of Grade 5, your child will
• Read longer texts fluently and with understanding, and write clearly for different purposes.
• Calculate with large numbers, fractions and measurements, and explain their method.
• Observe, question and explain how things work in the world around them.
• Work in a team, present to the class and take responsibility for their tasks.
Indian roots in the Preparatory Stage
• Mathematics problems set in Indian fairs, festivals and family life.
• Panchatantra, Jataka and folk tales in reading, used to discuss values such as honesty and kindness.
• Karnataka’s lakes, crops, foods and crafts as the first examples studied in EVS.
• Traditional games such as Chowka Bara and Ali Guli Mane for counting and strategy, and rangoli for patterns and symmetry.
• Indian music, dance and art forms in the arts.
How progress is checked
Short written assessments begin in this stage, alongside teacher observation, portfolios, projects, and self and peer reflection.

Academics: Middle Stage (Grades 6 to 8)
Thinking like mathematicians, scientists and historians.

In Grades 6 to 8, children meet each subject on its own terms. They learn to reason, investigate, analyse sources and argue with evidence, and they begin to take charge of their own learning. Mathematics, Science and Social Science are taught from the NCERT textbooks Ganita Prakash, Curiosity and Exploring Society: India and Beyond.

What children learn
Subject
What it covers
Mathematics
Patterns, number play and primes; fractions, decimals and integers; algebra with letter-numbers; perimeter, area, symmetry and constructions; squares, cubes and powers; proportional reasoning; data handling
Science
The diversity of living things; food and health; magnets, measurement and motion; materials, temperature and states of water; electricity and light; physical and chemical changes; the microbial world; forces and pressure; the particle nature of matter; the Earth, Moon, Sun and beyond
Social Science
Five themes across three years: India and the world, land and people; the tapestry of India’s past; our cultural heritage and knowledge traditions; governance and democracy; economic life around us
Languages
English, Hindi and Kannada: literature, grammar, and writing for real purposes, such as arguments, reports and reflections
Arts
Visual arts, music, dance and theatre
Physical education and well-being
Sport-specific skills, fitness, yoga and well-being
Skills for work
Hands-on projects in three kinds of work: growing living things, making with machines and materials, and serving people, through Ruchi
Computing and robotics
Spreadsheets, research skills, coding, programmable boards and how AI tools work
In the classroom
Children run investigations, collect data, work through case studies and debate questions using evidence. Each chapter ends with a project, a reflection or a real-world application.

By the end of Grade 8, your child will
• Reason with numbers, algebra and shapes, and explain their thinking.
• Plan a simple investigation, record observations and draw conclusions.
• Read maps, sources and timelines, and explain how India is governed.
• Write a clear argument, and speak in a discussion or debate.
• Complete a real project from start to finish and present it.
Indian roots in the Middle Stage
• Mathematics: Brahmagupta’s rules for zero, negative numbers and fractions, and the Virahanka sequence, as presented in the textbooks.
• Science: traditional Indian units of length, Acharya Kanad’s idea of the parmanu (atom), early Indian practice of variolation against smallpox, and Indian scientists such as J.C. Bose, alongside India’s space missions.
• Social Science: India’s cultural roots, unity in diversity, knowledge traditions and the story of Indian democracy.
• Arts: Indian dance and theatre forms, including Karnataka’s Yakshagana.
• Languages: Kannada and Hindi poetry and prose, read and discussed.
How progress is checked
Tests focus on reasoning and application, not only recall. They are combined with projects, experiments, a portfolio and at least one real-world project each term. The Summit Pathway begins in Grade 6.

Academics: Secondary Stage (Grades 9 and 10)
Depth, discipline and readiness for the board exam.

Grades 9 and 10 build deep understanding of each subject and prepare students for the CBSE Class 10 board examination. Students learn to analyse, apply concepts to unfamiliar problems and think critically, the skills that competency-based board questions now test.

Social Science
The Earth’s physical systems and India’s geography; Indian and world history; democracy, the Constitution and elections; economics, markets, development and personal finance

Preparing for the board
• Competency-based, board-pattern questions in every Grade 9 and 10 test.
• A planned revision calendar, timed practice papers and past-paper analysis, with detailed feedback.
• Preparation for both phases of the Class 10 board examination: the main exam and the optional improvement exam.
• Extra support for those who need it, and stretch work for those ready for more.
By the end of Grade 10, your child will
• Be fully prepared for the board examination.
• Apply concepts to real and unfamiliar problems, and explain their reasoning in writing.
• Analyse data, sources and case studies, and form a reasoned view.
• Know their strengths, and have a plan for choosing a stream after Grade 10.
Indian roots in the Secondary Stage
• Mathematics: the contributions of Baudhayana, Aryabhata, Brahmagupta, Bhaskara and Madhava, as featured in the NCERT textbooks.
• Science: Acharya Kanad’s parmanu, the acoustics of Karnataka’s Gol Gumbaz whispering gallery, and the work of C.V. Raman and other Indian scientists.
• Social Science: Indian knowledge systems in medicine, mathematics and astronomy; ideas of governance from Kautilya; the Constitution and Indian democracy.
• Languages: Kannada and Hindi literature.
Beyond the exam
The Summit Pathway continues with aptitude, current affairs, debate, national competitions, career conversations and stream guidance. The weekly Life Skill period builds practical skills for adult life.

Beyond the Classroom
Pulse: the heart of our week
A skill every week. For every child.

Pulse is our activity calendar: one period every week, for every class from Nursery to Grade 10, for a planned activity that builds one named skill. Each activity is linked to a chapter the class is studying, and roles rotate so every child takes part.

• Eight skill families: Communication · Collaboration · Creativity · Critical thinking · Leadership · Emotional and social skills · Physical well-being · Real-world skills
• Examples: a mystery bag in Nursery, a puppet show in Grade 2, “Be a News Anchor” in Grade 4, a design challenge in Grade 7, a budgeting simulation in Grade 10.
• A theme every month for the whole school, and a Pulse note home every month.
Ruchi: hobby hour
Find what you love. Then get good at it.

Ruchi means taste, or interest. Once a week, from Grade 1 to Grade 8, children regroup by hobby. Grades 1–2 try different areas, Grades 3–5 choose and explore, and Grades 6–8 build real skill in three kinds of work, as set out in NCERT’s Kaushal Bodh.

• Growing living things: kitchen garden, plant nursery, biodiversity explorers, hydroponics
• Making with machines and materials: robotics, maker skills, animation, AI builders, Channapatna-style toy making, textile crafts, pottery
• Serving people: fireless cooking, puppetry and storytelling, young curators, first-aid champions, water auditors, young media makers
• Arts: art, craft, photography, vocal music, flute, guitar, keyboard, drums, Bharatanatyam, Kathak, western dance
• Sport and mind: basketball, football, cricket, volleyball, table tennis, boxing, chess, quiz, yoga, traditional games
Kushala: life skills
Capable at school. Capable at home.

One practical life skill every month, practised at school and at home with a Kushala practice card. Examples: putting on shoes (Nursery), tying laces (Grade 1), basic first aid (Grade 3), reading an electricity bill (Grade 6), budgeting and safe online payments (Grade 10).

Aurora Lab, robotics and computing
Touch it. Test it. Build it. Code it.

• Aurora Lab, where ideas dawn: a hands-on science lab where children predict, test, get it wrong, think again and understand.
• Robotics, Grades 1–8: from simple moving models to programmable boards that solve local problems such as water, waste and traffic.
• Computing from Grade 1: digital basics, coding, data and AI, digital safety and creative media, in a curriculum we review every year.
Summit Pathway
Aim high.

In Grades 9–10 it adds aptitude practice, career conversations with professionals, and guidance on streams and entrance exams.

Frequently asked questions
Is ULL Auremont a CBSE school?
Yes, from Nursery to Grade 10, following the National Curriculum Framework (NCF 2023) and NEP guidelines.

Is ULL Auremont a new school?
No. ULL has served Nagarbhavi for [number] years. As ULL Auremont, its academic programme has been renewed from the ground up.

Which textbooks do you use?
NCERT textbooks for Mathematics, Science and Social Science from Grade 6. For Grades 1–5, and for languages, we use carefully chosen textbooks from established publishers, aligned with the NCERT curriculum.

Which languages are taught?
English is the medium of instruction. Hindi and Kannada are taught; Kannada from Grade 1 to Grade 10.

How important is sport at ULL Auremont?
Very. Sport is one of our four pillars. Every child has physical education, a fitness report card and house competitions, and talented children train for school teams and inter-school tournaments.

With all these activities, will academics suffer?

How do you prepare students for the Grade 10 board exam?
With board-pattern questions in every Grade 9–10 test, a revision calendar, timed practice papers, past-paper analysis and preparation for both board exam phases.

Do you prepare children for competitive exams?
The Summit Pathway, from Grade 6, builds the reasoning, reading, current affairs and debate skills that olympiads, competitive exams and the civil services rely on.

What does “Indian roots” mean? Is it religious?
It means Indian and Karnataka knowledge, language and examples are part of everyday lessons, alongside world knowledge. ULL Auremont is inclusive and does not give religious instruction.
